# IED Review: 10-20 Manual

## What this project is

Your reads become the reference answers that automated spike detectors are graded against. The project asks whether those detectors still work on EEG recorded under the scalp (subscalp EEG), where long-term home monitoring is possible.

- **Why subscalp EEG.** A small device under the scalp can record for months at home, but nobody yet knows how well spike detectors built for scalp EEG transfer to it.
- **Why expert reads.** There is no automatic ground truth for interictal epileptiform discharges (IEDs). Expert reads of short clips are the standard, and agreement between readers tells us how firm that standard is.
- **What we do with your answers.** Each answer is compared with the detectors on the same second, and with the other raters. Answers are analysed by clip, never by rater name in any report.

The clips come from long EMU recordings and include both clear events and ordinary background. Please read each clip on its own merits; the mix does not follow any fixed ratio.

## What you will do

The review has three parts, always in this order. Each part opens once you have answered every clip in the part before it.

1. **1,000 10-20 clips.** Each clip shows the full 10-20 scalp montage. These are the main part of the study, and this manual covers them.
2. **Ten training examples.** Ten spikes that an expert has already read. Each shows the 10-20 channels with the UNEEG channels underneath, so you can see how the same spike looks on both, with the kind of spike (generalized, or focal on the left or right) and a note on what shows it. You look through them; there is nothing to answer, and they are not scored.
3. **250 UNEEG clips.** Each clip shows only the three channels of the UNEEG device, recorded under the scalp.

The training examples and the UNEEG clips have their own manual, which opens when you start the training examples. The **Manual** button at the top of the page always opens the manual for the part you are in.

Clips come in batches of 25, so the whole review is about 50 batches. Most clips take a few seconds to read. Your answers save as you go, so you can stop at any point, close the page, and pick up where you left off from the same link. You do not need to finish a batch in one sitting.

## The question you answer

Each clip shows about ten seconds of EEG with one second in the middle shaded. For every clip you answer one question: **is there an epileptiform spike or sharp wave in the shaded second?** Answer Yes or No.

**Be flexible at the edges.** The shaded second is a guide, not a hard border. Count a spike if its peak falls inside the shaded second or within about a quarter of a second (250 ms) of either edge. This matters most just before the window: a spike that starts slightly before the shading and runs into it counts as Yes.

Answer No when:

- the only spike in view is clearly well outside the shaded second, more than about a quarter second from its edges;
- the shaded second holds only artifact, such as eye blinks, eye movement, muscle, electrode pops, chewing or movement;
- the shaded second holds normal sharp-looking activity, such as vertex waves, sleep transients, wickets, benign small sharp spikes or other normal variants;
- you are unsure whether what you see is epileptiform.

There is no expected number of Yes answers. Some batches may have many spikes and some may have none. Read each clip on its own.

## Using the review site

**Your link.** You get a personal link from the study lead. It signs you in, so there is no password. Open it in a desktop browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari) on a laptop or larger screen. The first page lists your batches and how many clips you have answered in each. Click a batch to start or continue it.

**The screen.** The traces fill most of the page, following EEG convention with negative up. Time is in seconds from the centre of the shaded second. The panel on the right holds your answer, an optional Note box, and Previous and Next buttons. A line near the top says when your last answer was saved.

**Keys.**

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Y | Yes, a spike in the shaded second, then the next clip |
| N | No, then the next clip |
| B, T, A | Double banana, transverse, or average reference montage |
| ↑ / ↓ | Larger or smaller traces (sensitivity steps, in µV/mm) |
| ← / → | Previous or next clip without answering |
| ? | Show the key list |

The **Manual** button at the top of the page opens this manual again at any time.

You can change the montage and the trace size as often as you like on any clip. They stay as you left them for the next clip.

**Changing an answer.** Go back with ← and press Y or N again. The newest answer is the one kept.

**Notes.** Use the Note box for anything worth flagging about a clip, such as a bad channel or a hard call. It is optional.

**Saving.** Each answer is sent to the study server as soon as you give it, and a copy is kept in your browser. The line at the top reads "Saved to server" with the time. If it reads "Not saved to server", keep the page open, since it retries on its own and your answers are still kept in the browser. If it stays that way, tell the study lead.

## Privacy and independence

The clips are de-identified. They carry no names, dates, record numbers or other details about the patient, and nothing in them links back to a person. Please keep it that way:

- **Keep your link to yourself.** It signs in as you, and anyone who has it can answer under your name.
- **No screenshots or recordings** of the clips, and no copying them off the site.
- **Read on your own.** Please don't discuss clips or your answers with other raters until everyone has finished. The study measures how often readers agree, so each answer needs to be your own.
- **Rate what you see.** You are not told anything about where a clip came from or why it was chosen, and the site will not show you other raters' answers. Please don't try to work these out.

Your answers are stored under your initials on the study server and are seen only by the study team.

## Common questions

**There are two spikes in the clip. What do I answer?** Answer Yes if either one has its peak in the shaded second or within about a quarter of a second of it. Spikes elsewhere in the ten seconds don't change the answer.

**A spike straddles the edge of the shading.** Answer Yes if its peak is within about a quarter of a second of the shaded second. Be generous with one that begins just before the shading.

**I'm not sure.** Answer No, and add a word in the Note box if it was a close call.

**I pressed the wrong key.** Press ← to go back and answer again. Only your newest answer is kept.

**A channel is flat or very noisy.** Answer on what the other channels show, and mention the bad channel in the Note box. If a whole clip is blank or won't load, move on with → and tell the study lead.

**Can I switch computers?** Yes. Open the same link on the other computer and your answers will be there.

**How long will it take?** About 50 batches of 25 clips, 40 of them 10-20 clips. Most people will want to spread it over several sittings.

**Who do I ask?** The study lead, who sent you your link. Please reach out with any question about the clips, the site or the study.
