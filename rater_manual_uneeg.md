# IED Review: UNEEG Manual

## What this part is

You have finished the 10-20 clips. The rest of the review reads the same kind of question on a different recording: EEG from the UNEEG device, a thin lead that sits under the scalp. This part has two steps, always in this order.

1. **Ten training examples.** Ten spikes that an expert has already read. Each shows the 10-20 channels on top and the UNEEG channels for the same seconds underneath, with the kind of spike and a note on what shows it. There is nothing to answer, and they are not scored.
2. **250 UNEEG clips.** Each clip shows only the three UNEEG channels. These are scored like the 10-20 clips.

The UNEEG clips open once you have looked at all ten examples. The **Manual** button at the top of the page opens this manual again at any time.

## The UNEEG device and its channels

The lead is placed under the scalp, outside the skull, behind one ear. It has three evenly spaced contacts a few centimetres apart: distal (D), centre (C) and proximal (P). The recording names them SQD, SQC and SQP.

[[figure: uneeg-lead]]

The clips show three bipolar channels made from these contacts, read like the pairs of a bipolar scalp montage:

- **D-C**, distal against centre, between neighbouring contacts;
- **P-C**, proximal against centre, between neighbouring contacts;
- **D-P**, distal against proximal. It spans both gaps, so its two contacts are twice as far apart as in the other two channels.

The three contacts sit over one small area of one side of the head, so these channels see much less of the brain than the 10-20 montage. A discharge far from the lead may not show on them at all, and one close to it can show clearly. Signals are smaller than on the scalp, so use ↑ to enlarge the traces. The montage keys (B, T, A) do nothing on these clips.

## How the training examples work

Each example shows about ten seconds with one second shaded, like the 10-20 clips. The 10-20 channels are on top and the three UNEEG channels are underneath, labelled UNEEG D-C, UNEEG P-C and UNEEG D-P.

1. The panel on the right shows the kind of spike (generalized, or focal on the left or right) and a note on what shows it.
2. Find it on the 10-20 channels, then look at the UNEEG channels underneath at the same moment.
3. Press → or Enter for the next example. There is nothing to answer, so take as long as you like.

Use the examples to learn how a spike on the scalp looks on the UNEEG channels: how much smaller it is, which of the three channels carry it, and what the background looks like around it. When you read the UNEEG clips you will not have the scalp to check against, so it helps to ask on each example whether you would have called it from the UNEEG channels alone.

## What to look for on the UNEEG channels

The question is the same as before: **is there an epileptiform spike or sharp wave in the shaded second?** The edge rule is the same too: count a spike if its peak is inside the shaded second or within about a quarter of a second (250 ms) of either edge.

An epileptiform discharge on these channels usually has most of these features:

- **A sharp, pointed peak** that stands out from the activity around it. A spike lasts about 20 to 70 ms and a sharp wave about 70 to 200 ms.
- **Different from the background.** It is clearly sharper, larger or of a different duration than the ongoing rhythm in the rest of the ten seconds, and it interrupts that rhythm rather than being part of it.
- **An uneven shape.** Often one side of the peak is steeper than the other.
- **An after-going slow wave.** A slower wave often follows the sharp part.
- **Seen on the channels together.** The three channels share their contacts, so anything real shows on at least two of them at the same moment. A discharge keeps the shape above on each channel that carries it.

Compare the shaded second with the rest of the clip. If the same shape repeats regularly through the ten seconds, or looks like the background, it is probably not a discharge.

## Common look-alikes on UNEEG

The lead sits behind the ear, close to the jaw muscles and near the neck, so some artifacts are more common here than on the scalp. Answer No to all of these.

- **Heartbeat (ECG).** A small sharp blip that repeats about once a second with the same shape each time. Check whether it lines up with the other blips in the clip; if it keeps the same rhythm, it is the heart.
- **Muscle.** Fast, spiky activity from chewing, clenching the jaw or talking, often in bursts that last a while. Individual spikes inside a muscle burst are too fast and too many to be discharges.
- **Movement and chewing.** Large, slow or rhythmic swings, often on all three channels together.
- **Electrode or contact pops.** A single very steep, step-like jump with no slow wave after it, on the two channels that use the same contact.

If the shaded second holds only artifact, answer No even if something sharp is there. If you are unsure whether what you see is epileptiform, answer No.

## Reading tips

- Start each clip at a size where the background is easy to see, then press ↑ if the signals look small. The size stays as you left it for the next clip.
- Read each UNEEG clip on its own, as if you had never seen any other clip. Don't try to recall a 10-20 clip.
- There is no expected number of Yes answers. Some batches may have many spikes and some may have none.
- Use the Note box for anything worth flagging, such as a noisy channel or a hard call.

## Common questions

**A spike shows clearly on the scalp in an example, but I can barely see it on UNEEG.** That is expected for some spikes, because the lead only sees a small area. The examples are there to show both cases.

**There is a regular sharp blip every second.** That is usually the heartbeat. Answer No unless something different and sharper sits in the shaded second.

**I'm not sure.** Answer No, and add a word in the Note box if it was a close call.

**I pressed the wrong key.** Press ← to go back and answer again. Only your newest answer is kept.

**Who do I ask?** The study lead, who sent you your link.

Everything in the 10-20 manual about privacy and reading on your own still applies: keep your link to yourself, take no screenshots, and don't discuss clips with other raters until everyone has finished.
